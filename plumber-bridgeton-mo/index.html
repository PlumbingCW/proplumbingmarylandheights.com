Around-the-clock dispatch

Plumbing emergencies in Bridgeton at any hour

A dispatcher answers the phone around the clock, so a flooded kitchen on a Sunday night is handled the same way as a clogged sink on a weekday. These four calls justify a night visit.

Sewer backup

Sewage in a tub, shower or floor drain means the main line is blocked. Stop all water use and call.

Burst or frozen pipe

A split in a supply line can drop hundreds of gallons across a floor. Close the main valve and call.

Water heater failure

A leaking tank or a unit with no hot water needs a plumber, not a long wait. Shut off the cold supply to a leaking tank.

Overflowing toilet

If the bowl keeps rising after a plunge, shut the supply valve behind it and call.

Main line blockages are cleared with sewer line repair when the pipe itself has failed, and with ordinary drain cleaning when it is just a clog. A camera inspection tells us which one you need before we dig or cut.

Housing age

Homes from the 1960s boom and the pipes inside them

Bridgeton's greatest residential growth came in the 1960s, when nearly 8,000 single-family homes and nearly 2,000 multi-family units went up. Plumbing installed in that era is now more than five decades old, and cast iron drains, clay laterals and older supply pipe all wear out on that kind of timescale. Ask your plumber which materials you have before budgeting any repair.

Multi-family construction kept going through the 1970s and 1980s, so Bridgeton also has apartment buildings and townhomes with shared drain stacks. A backup in a ground-floor unit can come from a blockage upstairs, so check with neighbors before you assume the problem is yours. Property managers should tell us how many units share the line when they call.

Our process

What happens on an emergency visit

We keep the order simple so you know what to expect from the moment the crew arrives.

  1. Stop the damage

    We locate the shutoff or the cleanout and stop the water or the backup before anything else.

  2. Find the cause

    A camera, a cable machine or a pressure check tells us whether the problem is a clog, a break or a failed fixture.

  3. Give a clear price

  4. Fix and test

    We run every affected fixture and confirm full flow before we leave.

Prepare

Before we arrive: protect floors and valuables

A few minutes of preparation saves flooring and electronics. Use the cards below while you wait.

1

Lift what you can

Pick up rugs, cardboard boxes and anything stored on a basement floor. Wet cardboard and carpet padding soak up sewage quickly.

2

Clear the way in

Open the garage or basement door and clear a path to the cleanout or the main valve. Landlords can leave a key with a neighbor.

3

Keep the toilet lid down

If sewage is rising, close lids and cover floor drains with a towel weighted down. Do not flush.

4

Note what happened

Write down which fixtures were slow before the backup. That detail helps us find the blockage quicker.
