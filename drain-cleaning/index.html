Emergency Drain Cleaning in Maryland Heights, MO

A backed-up sink, tub or floor drain does not wait for office hours, and neither does our dispatch line.

Kitchen and bath clogsMain line backupsCamera checks after clearing
Call (314) 461-6239

If water is standing in a tub, rising in a floor drain or coming back up the toilet, treat it as an emergency drain cleaning call. Our 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and we book same-day appointments when available for Maryland Heights homes and businesses.

While you wait, stop running water in the house, including the dishwasher and washing machine. Keep chemical drain cleaners out of the pipe, because they sit in a standing clog and can burn the hands of whoever opens the trap. If sewage is reaching the floor, keep children and pets out of the room and turn off power to anything sitting in the water.

Most clogs are cleared in a single visit with a cable machine, and we confirm the line is open before we pack up. When a clog keeps returning, a camera shows what is really going on inside the pipe, so you pay to fix the cause and not just the symptom.

Know the signs

When a clogged drain is an emergency

Not every slow drain needs a midnight call. The pattern of what is backing up tells you how urgent it is and where the blockage probably sits.

What you seeWhere the clog usually isHow urgent
One sink drains slowlyTrap or branch line for that fixtureSchedule soon, stop pouring grease or coffee grounds
Two or more fixtures slow, gurgling toiletsShared branch or the main drainCall today, the blockage is growing
Water or sewage rising in a tub or floor drainMain drain or sewer lateralEmergency, stop using water now
Washing machine drain overflowsStandpipe or laundry branchSame day if it floods the floor

A floor drain or a tub that fills when you flush the toilet means the blockage is downstream of every fixture. In that case the main drain is the problem, and sewer line cleaning or a longer machine is needed.

How we clear a drain and prove it is open

We start with the simplest tool that will work and move up only when the pipe tells us to. That keeps the visit short and the price fair.

  1. Find the access point

    We locate the closest cleanout, trap or vent opening so we do not have to pull a toilet unless the clog is right behind it.

  2. Run the cable

    A drum or sectional cable machine with the right head cuts through grease, hair and paper. Cables reach well past 50 feet on main line work.

  3. Check the pipe with a camera

    When a clog returns or the cable catches on something solid, a drain camera shows roots, a belly or a cracked joint before anyone digs.

  4. Flush and test

    We run water through every affected fixture and confirm full flow at the drain, not just at the sink we started on.

If the pipe is coated with grease or scale, the cable only opens a small hole. In that case hydro jetting washes the full wall of the pipe and is the better long-term fix.

Most common call

Kitchen sink clogs and what causes them

Kitchen lines clog differently from bathroom lines. Cooking grease cools inside the pipe and builds a waxy ring, and food scraps from a garbage disposal catch on that ring until water slows to a trickle.

If both sides of a double sink back up, the blockage is past the shared trap. If only the disposal side is full, the trouble is often the disposal outlet or the dishwasher connection. We check both before running a cable so we do not push a clog deeper into the wall.

A simple habit helps between visits. Wipe pans before washing, run hot water for a minute after the disposal, and keep fibrous scraps such as celery, corn husks and potato peels out of it.

Snaking, jetting or a camera: which one do you need?

The right tool depends on what is in the pipe and how often the clog comes back. The table below shows where each method fits.

MethodBest forLimit
Cable (snake)Single clogs from hair, paper, small rootsOpens a channel and can leave a coating on the wall
Hydro jettingGrease buildup, scale, repeated backupsNeeds a sound pipe and a cleanout for access
Camera inspectionClogs that return, unknown causes, pre-purchase checksShows the problem, does not clear it

A sewer camera inspection is worth the visit whenever a main line clog returns within a few months. A recurring clog almost always has a physical cause, and the camera shows it.

Local picture

Why drains back up in Maryland Heights

The sewer lateral, the private pipe that carries waste from your house to the main, is owned by the property owner according to the City of Maryland Heights. That means a root-filled or collapsed lateral is your repair, not the city's, though the city runs a lateral repair program funded by an annual fee on residential properties with six or fewer dwellings.

Under that program the city pays 100 percent of an approved repair up to $10,000 and 50 percent above that. If a clog turns out to be a broken lateral, our camera findings give you what you need to file a Sewer Lateral Claim Form with Public Works at (314) 291-6550.

Commercial properties around Westport Plaza and Riverport face a different problem. Restaurants and hotels send a lot of grease into their drains, so scheduled cleaning matters more than emergency calls. Our commercial plumbing crews can plan that work after hours.

Small drain leaks hide behind clogs

A drain line that leaks can look like a clog. Water stains under a sink, a musty cabinet floor or a damp spot on a basement ceiling under a bathroom can point to a cracked drain, a failed slip joint or a bad trap connection.

We check these joints while the line is open. If a leak is in the wall or under a slab, we move to leak detection so the opening is cut in the right place and not guessed at.

Clear price first

What affects the price of drain cleaning

We give a clear price before work starts. These factors move the number most.

Where the clog is

A fixture trap is quick work. A main line 60 feet from the cleanout takes longer machines and more time.

Pipe material and age

Cast iron with scale, clay with roots and PVC with a sag each behave differently on the cable.

Access

An exterior cleanout is the easiest route. Pulling a toilet to reach the line adds time.

Camera and jetting add-ons

A recording or a jetting pass costs more than a cable alone and often saves a repeat visit.

After-hours timing

Night and weekend dispatch carries a different rate than a weekday booking. You hear it before we start.

Codes and standards

What the codes say about drains and traps

Every fixture drain needs a trap and a vent under the plumbing codes, including the International Plumbing Code (IPC) that many Missouri cities use as a base. A slow drain that gurgles often points to a vent problem and not a clog, which is why we check the vent path before running a cable.

The City of Maryland Heights lists clearing a stoppage as ordinary repair that needs no permit. Relocating a drain does need one, so ask the building division before you move a fixture.
