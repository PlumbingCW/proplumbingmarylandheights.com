How to Install a Toilet Wax Ring in Maryland Heights, MO

A wax ring seals the toilet to the flange, and the flange height and condition matter as much as the ring itself.

By the Maryland Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

Call (314) 461-6239

To install a wax ring, shut off the water, drain the toilet and lift it off the closet bolts. Scrape the old wax from the flange and check that the flange is solid and level with the floor. Press a new ring onto the horn of the bowl or onto the flange, set the toilet straight down and tighten the nuts evenly. Then flush and check for leaks.

The hardest part is not the ring. It is the flange. A cracked, rusted or sunken flange will let even a new ring leak, and a flange that sits below the floor surface leaves a gap that wax cannot fill.

Below are the tools, the steps and the flange checks, followed by the signs that mean it is time to call a plumber and a note on local permits.

Check first

Signs the wax ring has failed

A failed seal shows up in a few ways

  • Water on the floor around the base, especially after flushing.
  • A toilet that rocks even slightly when you sit on it.
  • A sewer smell near the toilet that does not go away after cleaning.
  • A stain or soft spot in the floor around the base, or a ceiling stain below the bathroom.

Do not confuse a base leak with condensation or a leaking supply line. Dry the base, wrap a few sheets of toilet paper around the bottom and flush. If the paper is wet, the seal is the problem.

Tools and parts to have ready

Wax ring or wax-free seal

A standard ring fits most flanges. A thicker ring or a ring with a plastic horn helps if the flange sits slightly low.

New closet bolts

Reuse only if they are in good shape. New brass bolts and nuts are cheap and stop the toilet from shifting.

Putty knife and rags

To scrape the old wax from the flange and the base of the bowl.

Adjustable wrench and a sponge

To shut off the supply line, remove the nuts and sponge out the last water from the bowl.

Flange repair ring or extender

Only needed if the flange is cracked or low. Have one on hand before you lift the toilet.

Helper or a dolly

A toilet can weigh more than 70 pounds, and a second person keeps you from dropping it.

Replacing the ring: the steps

Take your time with the setting. The bowl goes down once and it should not be rocked back and forth.

  1. Shut off and drain

    Close the supply valve, flush and hold the handle, then sponge the last water out of the bowl and tank. Disconnect the supply line.

  2. Remove the toilet

    Take the caps and nuts off the closet bolts, rock the toilet gently to break the seal and lift it straight up. Set it on towels, not on its side.

  3. Clean the flange

    Scrape every trace of old wax off the flange and the bowl outlet. Stuff a rag in the drain opening to block sewer gas while you work.

  4. Check the flange

    It should be solid, round, level and at or slightly above the finished floor. Replace or repair it if it is cracked, rusted or loose.

  5. Set the new ring

    Press the ring onto the bowl outlet or the flange, install the closet bolts, then lower the toilet straight down with the bolts through the holes.

  6. Tighten evenly

    Add washers and nuts and tighten a little on each side, alternating. Stop when the toilet is firm. Overtightening cracks porcelain.

  7. Reconnect and test

    Reconnect the supply line, open the valve, let the tank fill and flush several times. Check around the base and the supply line for drips.

The flange: the part that decides whether it holds

When the toilet is off, look at the flange before you place the ring.

Flange conditionWhat it meansFix
Solid, level, at floor heightThe ring can seal normallyInstall a standard ring
Sits below the floor surfaceA gap that a standard ring cannot fillUse a thicker ring or add a flange extender
Cracked or rustedThe bolts cannot hold the toilet steadyFit a repair ring or replace the flange
Loose or spinning on the subfloorThe subfloor may be soft or damagedCall a plumber and check the floor

A subfloor that is soft or stained dark around the flange means the leak has been going on for some time. In that case the repair is bigger than a ring, and our toilet repair and installation crews can assess the flange and floor together.

Maryland Heights local note: toilet swaps and permits

The City of Maryland Heights lists replacing a toilet with no relocation as ordinary repair that does not need a plumbing permit. Resetting a toilet on a new wax ring falls in the same category, since the fixture stays where it is.

If your project moves the toilet, replaces the flange and part of the drain below the floor or adds a new bathroom, the work goes beyond a like-for-like swap. Ask the Community Development Building Division at (314) 291-6550 which rules apply before you open the floor.

Source: City of Maryland Heights, residential plumbing permits page (city government, Community Development Building Division).

When to call a plumber

Call a plumber if the flange is broken, if the floor is soft, if the toilet sits over a concrete slab with a damaged drain or if the toilet still leaks after a new ring. These problems often involve the pipe below the floor, and that is beyond a quick DIY.

If the toilet keeps clogging along with the leak, the problem may be farther down the line. Our plumbing installation crews can reset a toilet with new parts, and homeowners in Bridgeton and Hazelwood can call the same dispatch line as Maryland Heights residents.
