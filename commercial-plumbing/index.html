Right now

Emergency plumbing for a business: what to do first

The same steps limit damage in almost every commercial building, no matter how large the space.

The first ten minutes

  • Find the main shutoff valve, usually near where the water line enters the building, and close it.
  • Turn off power to wet areas at the breaker panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  • Stop using restrooms and sinks if a drain is backing up, because every flush adds more water to the overflow.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer and your landlord before anything is moved.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe the building, the location of the water and who is on site.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call Spire at (314) 621-6960 or 911 before you call a plumber.

Symptom first

Hidden leaks in commercial buildings

Commercial leaks rarely announce themselves. A water bill that climbs month after month, a damp patch on a slab in a storage room or a meter that keeps turning after closing all point to a leak somewhere inside or under the building.

We start with a simple meter test and then narrow the search with acoustic and electronic listening gear before any floor is opened. Our leak detection page explains how that pinpointing works. If the line runs under a parking lot or a slab, the goal is to open one small area and not guess.

Missouri American Water bills most properties in the city, and its customer line at 1-866-430-0820 can confirm whether the meter is registering usage while the building is closed.

Floor drain overflows and lift pump failures

Below-grade restrooms, kitchens and mechanical rooms depend on floor drains and pumps, and they fail in predictable ways.

Floor drain backs up

Grease, mop debris and roots in the branch line block the drain. We clear it with a cable or hydro jetting and camera the line if it returns.

Lift pump or ejector will not start

A stuck float, a failed motor or a clogged impeller keeps waste from leaving the pit. We pull the pump, clear the basin and test the alarm.

Sewage smell from a drain

A dry trap, a cracked line or a failing seal lets sewer gas into the space. We check the trap primer and the pipe before blaming the building.

Water around a pump basin

A bad check valve or a leaking basin lid lets water back into the room. We replace the failed part and run the pump through a full cycle.

Floor drains that serve a kitchen also need regular cleaning. Our drain cleaning crews can set a schedule so you are not calling in the middle of a Friday dinner service.

Grease trap cleaning for restaurants and kitchens

A grease trap catches fats before they reach the building sewer, and it only works while it has room. When it is full, grease passes through and coats the line, which is how restaurants end up with repeat backups.

We clean and inspect the trap, check the baffles and outlet, and note how quickly it fills so you can plan the next visit. If the line past the trap is already coated, jetting the run to the cleanout resets it.

Your landlord or lease may set cleaning intervals, and the sewer district or city can have its own rules. Ask the building division at (314) 291-6550 what applies to your kitchen.

Backflow devices and testing

Backflow devices keep contaminated water from flowing back into the public supply. Many commercial buildings have them on the fire line, the irrigation line or a boiler feed.

The City of Maryland Heights says a permit can be pulled without plans to install or replace a backflow device, and that repairing a backflow device does not need a permit. Testing schedules and who must perform them can differ by water system, so ask the city's building division which rules apply to your property before the test date arrives.

If a device has failed or is leaking, we can replace it as part of a planned install. Our plumbing installation page covers the planned side of that work.

What drives the price of a commercial plumbing job

We give a clear price before work starts. These factors move it the most.

Access and building type

A slab with no ceiling access takes longer than an exposed mechanical room.

Pipe size and material

Four-inch cast iron and half-inch copper are different jobs with different parts.

Timing

A 2 a.m. dispatch is priced differently from a planned Tuesday evening visit, and you hear the difference up front.

Permits and inspections

Where a permit applies, it becomes part of the schedule and the price.

Equipment on site

Jetters, cameras and pumps for large lines add cost, and they often prevent a repeat call.

Commercial property policies vary, and many cover sudden water damage but exclude wear and long-term seepage. Ask your insurer before assuming a loss is covered, and call our dispatch line for your real number.

Codes and standards

Codes that matter for a business

Commercial buildings add backflow prevention, grease interceptors and fixture counts to the usual plumbing rules. Reduced pressure backflow assemblies are made to ASSE 1013, and the building division decides which devices and tests apply to your use.

The City of Maryland Heights allows permits for backflow devices to be pulled without plans, so ask the building division at (314) 291-6550 before a restaurant or shop changes equipment.
