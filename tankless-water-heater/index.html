Tankless Water Heater Service in Maryland Heights, MO

Tankless units give continuous hot water, but they need correct venting, a properly sized gas or electric supply and regular descaling.

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows instead of storing it, which is why it can run out of tank-sized limits but not out of hot water. The trade-off is that it works harder per minute, so the gas line, vent or electric circuit has to be sized for the demand.

Maintenance

Flushing and descaling a tankless heater

Scale is the most common reason tankless heaters lose efficiency and throw error codes. Minerals bake onto the heat exchanger and narrow the water passages until flow drops.

  1. Isolate the heater

    The plumber shuts off the gas or power and closes the isolation valves that most units have on the hot and cold lines.

  2. Connect a pump and descaling solution

    A small pump circulates a food-safe descaling liquid through the heat exchanger. The loop runs long enough to dissolve scale.

  3. Flush and clean the filter

    Fresh water rinses the loop, and the inlet screen is cleaned or replaced.

  4. Restart and test

    The unit is brought back online, checked for flow and temperature and any stored error codes are reviewed.

How often a unit needs this depends on your water. The Missouri American Water 2021 report for its St. Louis County system shows hardness between about 4.4 and 9.9 grains per gallon at its Missouri River plants, and a home in that range often benefits from a yearly service. Test your tap for your own number.

Gas or electric tankless

Gas tankless heaters need a gas line that can deliver enough BTUs for the unit's input, plus a sealed vent to the outside. An older home with a half inch gas supply may need a larger line before a tankless unit can run at full output.

Electric tankless units draw a lot of current. A whole-house model may need multiple dedicated breakers, so the plumber looks at the electrical panel before quoting.

If you smell gas at any time, leave the building and call Spire at (314) 621-6960 or 911 before calling a plumber.

Replacing a tank with a tankless unit

What a tank-to-tankless swap usually needs

  • A gas line sized for the unit's input or an electrical panel that can feed it.
  • A sealed vent path to the outside that meets the manufacturer's rules.
  • A condensate drain for high-efficiency condensing units.
  • A wall space with clearance around the unit.
  • A plan for scale control, such as isolation valves and a descaling schedule.

Repairs on tankless units

Tankless repairs usually come down to scale, a failed sensor, a bad igniter or a venting problem. A plumber who has serviced these units knows which part to test first.

If the unit is old and the heat exchanger is badly scaled, replacement may cost less than repeated service. We explain both options before work starts.

Codes and standards

Standards and rules for tankless units

Gas tankless units are built to ANSI Z21.10.3 and need venting and gas piping sized to the manufacturer's installation standard. That is why a tankless swap often costs more than a tank swap, since the existing gas line and flue may not meet the new unit's requirements.

The permits page of the City of Maryland Heights does not spell out tankless gas work. Ask the building division at (314) 291-6550 what it requires before you buy a unit.
