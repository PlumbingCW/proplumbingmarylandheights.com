What to do first

When a leaking water heater is an emergency

A slow drip at a fitting can wait for a morning appointment. Water spreading across a floor cannot, because a full 40 or 50 gallon tank can empty in a few minutes once a seam gives way.

  1. Shut off the cold supply

    Turn the handle on the cold inlet pipe clockwise until it stops. If the valve is stuck, use the main shutoff where the water line enters the house.

  2. Cut the power or gas

    Flip the breaker for an electric heater, since heating elements burn out fast when the tank is empty. For gas, set the control to off and open a window if the room is closed in.

  3. Move what the water will reach

    Lift boxes, rugs and cardboard off the floor. Water finds floor drains, and it also finds the drywall bottom plate.

  4. Call and describe the leak

    Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from, such as the top, the side seam or the drain valve. That helps us bring the right parts.

If the tank is already empty and the house has no hot water, the next step is deciding between repair and water heater replacement, which the table below walks through.

Leak source

Where a puddle under the tank really comes from

Homeowners often find water at the edge of a garage slab or in a utility closet and assume the tank is finished. In practice, several different parts leak, and only one of them means a new heater.

Tank body or seam

Water running from the bottom or the side jacket means the steel tank has corroded through. This is not repairable, and replacement is the next step.

Inlet and outlet fittings

A weeping connection at the top is often a loose union or a failed flex connector. These are inexpensive fixes that take under an hour.

Temperature and pressure relief valve

The TPR valve and its discharge pipe release water when pressure or heat climbs. A steady drip can mean high water pressure or a worn valve.

Drain valve

The brass or plastic valve near the bottom can drip after a flush. Replacing it takes a short visit if the tank is otherwise sound.

Condensation

A cold tank in a warm room can sweat, and a high-efficiency gas unit makes condensate by design. Check that the puddle is actually clear and not rusty.

A puddle that appears at the slab edge near the garage may travel from a leak elsewhere. If the heater is dry on top and the floor stays wet, our leak detection crew can trace the supply line before anyone replaces the wrong thing.

No hot water or lukewarm water: what usually failed

When the shower runs cold, the symptom narrows the list quickly. The two columns below match what we see most often on gas and electric tanks.

SymptomGas tank, likely causeElectric tank, likely cause
No hot water at allPilot out, bad thermocouple or failed gas valveTripped breaker, burnt upper element or failed upper thermostat
Water warms then turns cold fastSediment layer blocking the burnerLower element failed or dip tube broken
Water is hot but runs out quicklySediment or undersized tank for the householdOne element working, the other dead
Popping or rumbling noiseScale boiling under the sediment layerScale on the elements
Rusty or smelly hot waterAnode rod used upAnode rod used up

Many of these parts are routine repairs. If the tank is leaking from the body, or if it has already needed repeated repairs, a new unit is the sound choice.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters fail in different ways

A gas water heater adds combustion to the list of things that can go wrong. The burner, the flue draft and the gas control valve are all safety components, so a plumber checks venting and flame quality before calling the repair done.

Electric tanks are simpler. They fail at the elements, the thermostats and the high-limit reset button, and a multimeter tells us which part is dead within minutes.

Hybrid heat pump models combine a compressor with backup elements. They need more clearance and a drain for condensate, and they are covered best by a technician who has worked on them before. Tell the dispatcher the make and model if you know it, because it changes which parts we carry.

Local picture

What Missouri River water does to a tank

Water chemistry and sediment

Missouri American Water supplies Maryland Heights. Its 2021 water quality report for the St. Louis County system lists total hardness at its Missouri River plants between 76 and 170 ppm as calcium carbonate, which is roughly 4.4 to 9.9 grains per gallon.

Water in that range deposits scale on heating elements and settles as sediment at the bottom of a tank. That is why tanks in this area often show popping noises and shortened element life. If you want your own number, test your tap, since the 2021 figures describe the utility's plants and not your pipes.

Flushing a tank once a year clears loose sediment and extends element life. If your water is on the hard side, a water softener protects the heater and the fixtures.

What affects the price of a water heater repair

You get a clear price before work starts. These factors move the number.

The failed part

A thermostat or element is a smaller job than a gas control valve or a relief valve with corroded threads.

Tank age and condition

A sound tank is worth a repair. A tank with a rotted bottom is not.

Fuel type and venting

Gas work includes checking the flue and combustion air, and electric work includes testing the circuit.

Access

A heater in a tight closet or a crawlspace takes longer to service than one in an open basement.

Codes and standards

What the standards say about water heater safety

Gas-fired storage water heaters are built to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard and electric tanks to UL 174, and both require a temperature and pressure relief valve. A relief valve that drips is doing its job, and one that never lets go is a problem the tank cannot solve for itself.

The City of Maryland Heights allows permits for water heaters up to 120 gallons to be pulled without plans. Repairs that do not replace the tank need no permit, so ask the building division if the job grows.
