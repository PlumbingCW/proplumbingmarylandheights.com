Slow Draining Sink That Is Not Clogged in Maryland Heights, MO

A sink that drains slowly without a full clog almost always has a partial blockage, a vent problem or a film inside the pipe.

By the Maryland Heights Pro Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026

Call (314) 461-6239

Water that sinks slowly but does drain is the early stage of a clog. Hair, soap scum, toothpaste and grease coat the inside of the trap and the pipe behind it, narrowing the path a little at a time. The sink never blocks completely, so people live with it for months.

Start at the stopper and the trap, because those two parts cause most slow bathroom and kitchen drains. If the sink still crawls after you clean them, look at whether other fixtures are slow too, since that points at the main line.

This guide covers the bathroom and kitchen versions separately, then shows how to tell a vent problem from a blockage.

Likely causes

Five reasons a sink drains slowly with nothing stuck

A coating inside the trap

Soap, toothpaste and hair form a sticky lining. The opening shrinks until water barely passes.

A pop-up stopper holding hair

The stopper rod and its pivot collect a mat of hair just below the drain opening.

Grease film in a kitchen line

Warm grease cools and hardens inside the pipe, leaving a rough wall that catches food.

A partly blocked vent

Plumbing vents let air in behind the water. If the vent is blocked, the drain slows and gurgles.

A restriction in the shared branch or main

When several fixtures run slow, the shared pipe downstream is narrowed or sagging.

Bathroom sink: the stopper and the first six inches

In a bathroom sink, the problem is usually within an arm's reach of the drain. Lift out the stopper, wipe off the hair and slime, and clean the pivot rod behind the tailpiece. A small plastic drain tool or a pair of needle-nose pliers pulls out the mat that a cable would only push deeper.

If the stopper is clean and the sink is still slow, place a bucket under the trap, unscrew the slip nuts and rinse the trap into the bucket. Reassemble with the washers seated, and run water while you watch for drips at both nuts.

Avoid pouring chemical cleaner into a slow sink. It sits on top of a partial clog and can splash back or soften the trap's plastic, and it does not cut through hair at all.

Kitchen sink: grease film and the disposal side

Kitchen lines carry grease that bathrooms never see. Even with hot water and soap, fat cools two or three feet down the pipe and builds a thin wall of wax, which food scraps then catch on.

If you have a double sink, test each basin. When only the disposal side is slow, the problem often sits at the disposal outlet or the dishwasher connection, which a garbage disposal repair visit can check. When both basins are slow, the trap arm or the wall branch is narrowed.

A good habit is to run hot water for a minute after the disposal and wipe greasy pans before washing them. It will not clear an old film, but it slows a new one.

How a vent problem sounds and feels

Plumbing vents are easy to forget until they fail. A blocked vent changes how the drain behaves in ways a plain clog does not.

What you noticePlain partial clogVent problem
Gurgling after the water leavesRareCommon
Slow drain only when other fixtures runSometimesCommon
Water level drops in a smooth, slow slideYesOften jerky, with air bubbles
Sewer odor in the bathroomRareMore likely
Cleaning the trap helpsYesOnly briefly

Vents exit through the roof, so a plumber should check them with a camera or a smoke test rather than you climbing up. If the symptoms match the right column, book drain cleaning with a vent check.

When the slowness is really the main line

Signs the problem is bigger than one sink

  • A shower or tub drains slowly at the same time as the sink.
  • The toilet gurgles when you run the sink or washing machine.
  • A floor drain or the lowest fixture in the house bubbles or shows water.
  • The slowness returns within days of clearing a trap.

Those signs point to a shared pipe or the sewer lateral. At that stage a sewer camera inspection shows what is narrowing the line, and it is cheaper to find out early than after a backup.

Maryland Heights local note: who handles what

The City of Maryland Heights lists clearing stoppages, replacing a sink or faucet with no relocation and stopping leaks as ordinary repair that does not need a permit. That means a trap cleanout or a faucet swap on a slow sink is something you or a plumber can do without paperwork.

The sewer lateral that serves your house is owned by the homeowner according to the city. If the slow drain turns out to be a root-filled or collapsed lateral, the repair is yours, and the city's lateral repair program can help with approved repairs. Public Works at (314) 291-6550 can explain how to apply.
