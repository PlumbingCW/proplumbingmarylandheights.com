Neighborhood

Main Street, the Historic District and old-building plumbing

St. Charles was founded around 1769 as Les Petites Cotes and became a city in 1809. The Riverfront and Main Street lie within the St. Charles Historic District, where restored buildings now house restaurants and specialty shops. Old buildings often have walls and floors that were never designed for modern kitchens, so a camera inspection before any cutting saves time and repair cost.

Restaurants in these buildings send grease through lines that may be decades old. Regular jetting keeps those lines open, and a camera shows the condition. Ask us about a schedule if your kitchen has had more than one backup.

Cross-river note

Different county, different rules

St. Charles is in St. Charles County, not St. Louis County. The sewer lateral fees that Maryland Heights and Chesterfield collect on St. Louis County tax bills do not automatically apply here, so ask the city whether any repair program exists before you pay for a large job.

The City of Saint Charles also supplies its own drinking water from the Elmpoint well field and publishes a Water Main Breaks map. If every tap in your house loses pressure at once, check that map and call City Hall at (636) 949-3200 before assuming the problem is inside your walls.

Codes and standards

Codes and rules to check in St. Charles

St. Charles is in a different county from Maryland Heights, so its permits, utility and rules are its own. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) is the model most cities start from, but ask St. Charles which edition it enforces before replacing a heater or moving a fixture.

Ask for the pipe and fitting standard in writing on any repipe or sewer job. Copper tube is made to ASTM B88, PEX to ASTM F876 and PVC sewer pipe to ASTM D3034, and a quote that names them is easier to compare.
