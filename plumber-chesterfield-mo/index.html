Around-the-clock dispatch

Chesterfield emergencies by season

Dispatch runs around the clock because storms and freezes do not keep office hours. Each season brings a different pattern.

Spring rain

Sump pumps work hardest and fail most often when the ground is saturated. A pump that runs constantly or not at all needs attention.

Summer storms

A heavy downpour can overwhelm sewer lines and push water through floor drains.

Fall leaf season

Roots and debris collect in older laterals. Slow drains in October often turn into backups by December.

Winter freezes

Supply lines in exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces split when temperatures drop and thaw.

Basement water starts with sump pump repair, burst lines with burst pipe repair, and floor drain backups with drain cleaning. If the sewer line itself has failed, sewer line repair is the next step.

Local history

Chesterfield Valley, the 1993 flood and your basement

On July 30, 1993, around 10:15 p.m., the Monarch levee failed with the Missouri River more than 19 feet above flood stage. Floodwater stood 6 to 15 feet deep across about 4,000 acres of Chesterfield Valley, which had not flooded since 1935. If your home or business is in the Valley, a working sump pump and a backwater valve on the sewer line are the two checks worth making before the next wet season.

The levee has since been rebuilt as the Monarch-Chesterfield Levee, designed for a 500-year event, and Chesterfield Commons was built afterward. A rebuilt levee protects against river flooding, but it does nothing for a pump that has failed or a drain that backs up from the street. Test the pump by pouring a bucket of water into the pit, and call us if it does not start.

Prepare

Before we arrive: protect the basement and the pump

A flooded basement is a race against damage. These four steps matter most.

1

Kill power safely

If water is near outlets, switch off breakers from a dry spot only. If you cannot reach the panel safely, call and wait.

2

Check the pump

Look at the sump pit from a dry spot. A pump that hums but does not move water, or sits idle while the pit overflows, tells us what to bring.

3

Lift valuables

Move boxes, electronics and furniture onto blocks or upstairs. Wet drywall and insulation spread damage fast.

4

Find your bill

Keep your water and tax bills handy. They help confirm your provider and your lateral program status.

Codes and standards

Codes and rules to check in Chesterfield

Chesterfield runs its own sewer lateral program and its own permit office, so the rules on this page are the city's and not Maryland Heights's. The International Plumbing Code (IPC) is the usual model, and the city decides which edition applies, so confirm before work beyond a repair.
