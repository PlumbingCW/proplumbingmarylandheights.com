About Maryland Heights Pro Plumbing

We answer plumbing emergencies in Maryland Heights, Missouri, and we tell you the price before the work starts.

Call (314) 461-6239

What we do

Maryland Heights Pro Plumbing is a 24/7 plumbing line for Maryland Heights and the towns around it. You call, describe the problem and we send licensed, insured plumbers who handle everything from a slow sink to a failed sewer line.

We work on homes, apartments and small businesses. Our services run from drain cleaning and water heater replacement to sump pump repair and whole house repiping.

How we work

Clear price first

You hear the price before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.

Diagnosis before repair

We find the cause with the right tool, such as a camera or a pressure test, before anything is cut open.

Honest options

When repair and replacement are both reasonable, we explain the trade-offs and let you choose.

Local rules respected

Where a permit applies we say so. The City of Maryland Heights lists what needs one on its permits page.

What we will not do

We do not promise an arrival time we cannot keep, and we do not publish prices for jobs we have not seen. Plumbing costs depend on access, materials and timing, so the honest answer comes after you describe the problem.

We also avoid scare tactics. If a repair can wait until morning, we say so, and if a leak is slow enough to schedule for a weekday, you will hear that too.

How to check any plumber before you hire

These steps work for us and for anyone else you call.

  • Ask for the price in writing or by text before work starts.
  • Ask who will do the work and whether they are licensed and insured.
  • Ask the Maryland Heights building division at (314) 291-6550 which jobs need a permit and who may pull it.
  • Be wary of anyone who quotes a total over the phone without hearing the symptoms.

What happens when you call

The call follows the same short path every time, whether it is a leak at midnight or a water heater that stopped heating on a Sunday.

  1. Describe what the water is doing

    Tell the dispatcher where it is coming from and whether you have already found the shutoff valve.

  2. Safety questions first

    We ask about gas smells, standing water near electrical items and whether anyone is at risk. Those answers decide the order of everything else.

  3. Hear the price before work starts

    A plumber explains what is wrong, what the fix involves and what it costs, and you decide whether to go ahead.

  4. Confirm it works

    Before the crew leaves, water is run through the repaired fixture or line to confirm it does what it should.

Licensed and insured, and how to verify it

We say licensed and insured because the plumbers who go to your house are. We do not publish license numbers or insurer names on this site, so ask for them on the phone and write them down.

A reasonable plumber answers that question without hesitation. If the answer is vague, or the quote changes once the truck is in the driveway, call the Maryland Heights building division at (314) 291-6550 and ask what it expects from a contractor on your job.

Local rules we work around

According to the City of Maryland Heights, ordinary repairs such as clearing a stoppage or stopping a leak do not require a permit. It does allow permits to be pulled without plans for water heaters up to 120 gallons, backflow devices, water lines and sewer laterals.

That matters when a job grows. A drain call that turns into a lateral repair, or a water heater swap that turns into a bigger tank, can move from no permit to a permit, and we tell you when that happens so it does not surprise you.

You can read the city's own list on its permits page, and the building division will confirm by phone. We would rather you hear about a permit from us before the work starts than from an inspector afterward.

Homes, apartments and small businesses

A house, a rental unit and a restaurant kitchen fail in different ways. A house usually has one main line and one water heater, while an apartment building shares stacks and risers, and a business may have grease traps, floor drains and a lift pump.

Tell the dispatcher which kind of building it is. For shared lines and tenant calls, our commercial plumbing page explains how we handle property managers and after-hours access.
