Emergency Sewer Line Cleaning in Maryland Heights, MO

When the main line is blocked, every drain in the house is affected and the lowest fixture floods first.

Main line clearingRoot cuttingCleanout access
Call (314) 461-6239

If the lowest drain in your house is filling with wastewater, the main sewer line is probably blocked, and that is an emergency. Our 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and same-day appointments are offered when available. Until we arrive, stop using water, leave the cleanout cap on unless you know how to release pressure safely, and keep people away from the wet area.

Main line cleaning uses heavier machines than a sink cable. We use sectional or drum cables with cutting heads for roots and, when the pipe is coated, a water jet to finish the job.

Afterward we run a camera down the line when the pipe, the history or your questions call for it, so the backup is explained and not just cleared.

Emergency

Sewage in the lowest drain: an emergency right now

What helps while you wait

Fixtures that sit lowest, such as a basement shower, a laundry drain or a floor drain, show a main line blockage first. If those are filling or toilets gurgle when another fixture drains, wastewater has nowhere to go but back into the house.

Do not run water to see if it clears. Every gallon you add comes up somewhere. Put towels or a wet vacuum to work only if the electrical situation is safe, and call the line so a crew can come out the same day when one is available.

Find your cleanout before the next backup

A cleanout is a capped access pipe on the sewer line. Knowing where yours is turns a long visit into a short one.

  1. Check outside near the foundation

    Look for a 3-inch or 4-inch capped pipe sticking out of the ground or a small flush cap in the yard, often on the side facing the street.

  2. Check the basement and utility area

    A cap near the floor of a basement, garage or utility room can be the access point for the main drain.

  3. Clear a path to it

    Move shrubs, stored items and soil so a crew can reach it with hoses and cables.

  4. Tell dispatch what you found

    Describe the location and whether the cap turns by hand, and we will bring the right tools.

How often a main line needs cleaning

There is no honest fixed interval that fits every house. A newer PVC lateral under a lawn with no trees may never need routine cleaning, while an older line near large trees can fill with roots again within a short time.

The camera is what tells you. If the footage shows a smooth pipe, we say so and recommend nothing. If it shows roots at a joint, we explain whether periodic cleaning or a joint repair costs you less over time.

Pipe material, age, slope and nearby trees all play a part, and so does what goes down the drain. Grease, wipes marketed as flushable and kitchen scraps are the usual household contributors.

A different line for businesses: grease traps and floor drains

Restaurants, hotels and kitchens around Westport Plaza and Riverport often have grease traps or interceptors that need scheduled pumping and cleaning. A full trap pushes grease into the main line, which is how a kitchen ends up with a repeat sewer backup.

Our commercial plumbing team can plan trap cleaning, floor drain service and main line jetting outside business hours so the dining room stays open.

Storm drains and yard drains are a separate system

Know which pipe is flooding

The City of Maryland Heights maintains public streets and city storm sewers, and Public Works takes after-hours calls at (314) 298-8700. If water is ponding at a street inlet, that is the city's system and not your sewer line.

A private yard drain, a downspout line or a basement perimeter drain on your property is a different matter. Those lines are yours, and we can clear them, though they follow different rules from a sanitary sewer lateral.

What affects the price of sewer line cleaning

Where the blockage sits

A clog near the cleanout is quick. One near the street connection requires full hose length.

What it is made of

Soft paper clears fast. Dense roots or hardened grease need heavier tools.

Pipe material

Cast iron, clay and PVC each limit how aggressively we can cut.

Camera recording

Video adds time and gives you evidence for the city program or a buyer.

Codes and standards

Standards for describing what the camera finds

After a main line is cleared, the camera footage is only useful if the defects are described consistently. The NASSCO PACP coding system is the published standard for naming cracks, roots, offsets and fractures, and it lets a second plumber read your report without guessing.

Ask for the footage and the notes after a cleaning. If roots or a belly show up, that record is the starting point for a repair decision.
